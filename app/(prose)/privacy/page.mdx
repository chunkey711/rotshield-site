# Privacy Policy

## The short version

Your Screen Time data never leaves your iPhone: not which apps you block, not how long you use them, not the per-app numbers on the Today screen. Rotshield has no account and no server of its own.

Two things do leave the phone, starting with version 1.1:

- **Usage analytics** — which screens you open and which steps you take in the app, plus masked screen recordings of the app itself, sent to PostHog in the EU. They tell me where people get stuck. You can turn them off in Settings → Share usage analytics.
- **Subscription status** — whether you have an active subscription, handled by RevenueCat so the app can unlock and keep working after a renewal. Apple takes the payment; I never see your card or your Apple ID.

Neither is linked to your name, email or Apple ID, and neither is used for advertising or to track you across other apps.

## What Rotshield collects

### Usage analytics

When analytics are on, the app sends events like these to PostHog:

- which screen you opened, and which onboarding step you reached, including which question of the onboarding quiz;
- your answers to the onboarding questions — the options you tapped, including your age range and roughly how many hours a day you think you spend on your phone. Never free text;
- whether you granted Screen Time and notification permissions, or skipped the notification step; if Screen Time permission fails, the error message iOS returned;
- that you opened Apple's app picker and how many apps and categories you chose — a count, not which ones — or that you skipped creating a rule;
- that you created, paused, resumed or deleted a rule, or that creating it failed; its type, how many apps and categories it covers, and the limit length in minutes;
- that you saw the subscription screen, whether prices loaded, which plan you picked, whether a purchase started, finished, was cancelled or failed (with the error code), and whether you tapped Restore;
- the steps of the "unlock" challenge, and whether it ended with an access window or you gave up.

Each event carries a random identifier generated on your phone, your device model, iOS version, app version and language. PostHog sees your IP address when the event arrives.

**Screen recordings.** When analytics are on, the app also records what its own screens look like, so I can see where people get confused. Every piece of text and every image is masked before the recording leaves the phone, and the screens that show Screen Time data — the per-app report, app icons, and Apple's app picker — are blanked out completely. Nothing outside Rotshield is recorded.

**Turning it off.** Settings → Share usage analytics. Events stop immediately, recordings no later than the next app start, and both stay off until you turn them back on.

What I never send, analytics on or off: which apps you picked, app names or icons, how long you used anything, the numbers in the Screen Time report, rule names, or what you type into a check-in.

### Subscriptions

To know whether you have paid, the app uses RevenueCat. It receives from Apple and from the app:

- your purchase history for Rotshield — which plan, when it started, renews or ends, refunds;
- a random identifier the app generates for you, and the analytics identifier above, so that a renewal and the screens before it can be matched;
- your country, price and currency, as reported by Apple.

When you install Rotshield from an Apple Ads advert, iOS gives the app an anonymous attribution token that RevenueCat uses to tell which ad campaign led to the install. This works without asking to track you and does not use your advertising identifier (IDFA).

Payment itself happens entirely inside Apple's App Store. I never see your card, your name, your email or your Apple ID.

## What stays on your device

Rotshield keeps its state in a private app group container on the phone (`group.com.ruslan.rotshield`). It never leaves the device.

- **Your rules** — name, type (schedule, all day, or daily limit), days of the week, times, limit length, on or off.
- **Which apps a rule covers** — opaque tokens, not app names. See below.
- **Settings** — your daily screen time goal, your answers to the onboarding questions, whether analytics are on, and a few flags that remember what you have already been asked or shown, including whether your subscription was active the last time the app checked.
- **Focus timer** — the running session and when it ends, plus the durations you used recently.
- **Access window** — while a 15-minute window is open: which rules it covers and when it closes.
- **Check-ins** — if you take the "why open it?" challenge: the reason you picked, the note you typed, the mood number, and a timestamp. The last 200 are kept; older ones drop off.
- **Counters** — how many times you turned back today, and which screen time thresholds today has crossed.

You can change or remove any of this inside the app — edit a rule, delete a rule, end a session. To erase all of it at once, delete Rotshield from your phone: iOS destroys the container, and everything above goes with it.

## About the apps you block

You choose them in Apple's own picker, and iOS hands the app back opaque tokens — not names, not bundle identifiers, not icons. Rotshield cannot tell which apps you picked. When you see an app's name or icon on a shield or in a rule, the system is drawing it, not the app.

## About your screen time numbers

The per-app breakdown on the Today screen is drawn by a `DeviceActivityReport` extension — a small, separate, sandboxed process that Apple runs to display Screen Time data.

Apple's sandbox does not let that extension write its results anywhere: not to a file, not to shared storage, not over the network. The numbers are computed, drawn on screen, and discarded. The main app never receives them, so neither do I, and neither does anyone else. That part of the screen is also blanked out of the screen recordings described above.

The one number the app itself knows is a rough daily total. Rotshield asks iOS to tell it when your screen time passes 15 minutes, 30, 45, and so on, and remembers the highest threshold crossed today. That marker is stored on the device and is never sent anywhere.

## Notifications

Rotshield sends local notifications only — scheduled and delivered by iOS on your phone. There is no push server, and the app never registers for a push token.

There are three: your 15-minute window has closed, your focus session is finished, and a tap-through when you press "Unlock" on a shield. Notifications are optional, and blocking works exactly the same if you decline them.

## Apple

Rotshield is distributed through the App Store. When you download it or subscribe, Apple collects what Apple collects for any download or purchase. Screen Time permission is granted to Apple's system frameworks, not to me. You can withdraw it at any time in iOS Settings, under Screen Time.
